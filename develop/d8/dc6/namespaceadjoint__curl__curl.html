Detailed Description

The Karniadakis-Israeli-Orszag splitting of Neko's \( P_N-P_N \) scheme puts the rotational form of the viscous term, evaluated on the extrapolated velocity \( \mathbf{u}_e \), into the pressure residual (pnpn_prs_res). Before gather-scatter, that contribution is

\[ r_{cc}(\mathbf{u}_e) = -\frac{\mu}{\rho} G^T M \mathbf{W}, \qquad \mathbf{W} = M D_c M D_c \mathbf{u}_e, \]

where \( D_c \) is the element-local, pointwise curl, \( M \mathbf{y} = \bar{B}^{-1} \mathrm{gs}(B \mathbf{y}) \) is the mass-weighted average that Neko's curl applies to it (so that curl is \( M D_c \)), with \( B \) the element-local diagonal mass matrix and \( \bar{B} = \mathrm{gs}(B) \) the assembled one (coefBinv holds \( \bar{B}^{-1} \)), and \( G \) is the weak gradient opgrad, \( G_i p = B D_i p \), whose transpose is cdtp. Since \( \bar{B} = \mathrm{gs}(B) \), \( M \) maps every field to a continuous one and leaves continuous fields unchanged, so it is a projection, \( M^2 = M \), and

\[ r_{cc}(\mathbf{u}_e) = -\frac{\mu}{\rho} G^T M D_c M D_c \mathbf{u}_e . \]

The adjoint of this term with respect to \( \mathbf{u}_e \), acting on the adjoint pressure \( p^\dagger \), therefore carries two factors \( M^T \) and is the volume load

\[ \mathbf{L} = \frac{\mu}{\rho} D_c^T M^T D_c^T M^T G p^\dagger, \qquad M^T \mathbf{y} = B \, \mathrm{gs}(\bar{B}^{-1} \mathbf{y}), \]

i.e. for every continuous \( \mathbf{u}_e \) and \( p^\dagger \), \( \mathbf{u}_e \cdot \mathbf{L} = -p^\dagger \cdot r_{cc}(\mathbf{u}_e) \), with both dot products summed over all local (non-unique) nodes and neither \( \mathbf{L} \) nor \( r_{cc} \) gather-scattered.

The transpose is exact in three dimensions only. For a two-dimensional mesh Neko's curl drops the \( z \) derivatives and does not mass-average the \( z \) component, whereas \( \mathbf{L} \) applies the full three-dimensional \( D_c^T \) and \( M^T \) to all three components. The adjoint case therefore rejects two-dimensional meshes, and adjoint_curl_curl_check rejects the boundary conditions for which the load is not the transpose of the primal term.

In the continuous setting \( \nabla \times \nabla p^\dagger = 0 \) and the term reduces to a boundary integral. The discrete operators do not satisfy that identity, so a boundary-only form is not the transpose of what the primal computes; the load acts on the whole volume.

The load does not transpose the symmetry-surface part of the primal term (bc_sym_surface), nor the cyclic rotations applied inside the primal's curl. The symmetry test is reduced over all ranks, so that every rank stops, also those that hold no symmetry facet. The restriction to three-dimensional meshes (see the module description) is not tested here: it has to be enforced before the adjoint fluid is initialised, which assumes a three-dimensional mesh.

See the module description for the notation. The load is element-local (not gather-scattered), since it is added to the adjoint forcing, which the velocity residual gather-scatters. It is added to the forcing before makeabf, which scales the forcing by \( \rho \) and extrapolates it in time, so the velocity right-hand side receives \( \mu D_c^T M^T D_c^T M^T G p^\dagger \), weighted over the lagged adjoint pressures as the primal's extrapolation weights \( \mathbf{u}_e \). The material properties are taken as constant, as in the primal residual. The cyclic rotations and the symmetry-surface part of the primal term are not transposed, so the load is not valid for cyclic or symmetry boundaries (see adjoint_curl_curl_check), nor for a two-dimensional mesh. All field arguments must be distinct.
